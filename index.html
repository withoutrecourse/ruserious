Common Law Foundation — All 50 States

These rights arise from judge-made common law adopted universally across all US jurisdictions. They do not depend on any single state legislature. No internet connection required to assert them. No lawyer required to invoke them.

Natural
Law

Inherent Rights of Man — Bodily autonomy and freedom from unconsented surveillance are pre-political, fundamental rights recognized under natural law and the founding documents of the United States.

§ 652B

Intrusion Upon Seclusion — Intentional intrusion into a private matter that a reasonable person would find highly offensive. Smart glasses recording without notice meets this threshold.

Restatement (Second) of Torts § 652B — adopted in all US jurisdictions
§ 652C

Appropriation of Likeness — Use of another's name, image, or biometric identity without written consent. Facial geometry captured by AI glasses is likeness data.

Restatement (Second) of Torts § 652C
§ 652D

Public Disclosure of Private Facts — Sharing private information a reasonable person would object to having shared or retained.

Restatement (Second) of Torts § 652D
§ 652E

False Light — Placing another in a false light through disclosure or misuse of recorded information.

Restatement (Second) of Torts § 652E

Georgia — O.C.G.A. § 16-11-62

O.C.G.A. § 16-11-62 — Unlawful Surveillance

Unlawful to use any device to observe, photograph, videotape, record, or transmit the image of another without consent where a reasonable expectation of privacy exists. Criminal + civil exposure.

O.C.G.A. § 16-11-60 et seq. — Georgia Electronic Surveillance Act

Covert recording of private conversations without consent is prohibited. Misdemeanor on first offense; felony exposure if recording with intent to distribute.

O.C.G.A. § 51-1-6 — Civil Remedy

Statutory violations give rise to civil tort claims including actual damages, punitive damages, and attorney's fees.

Illinois — BIPA 740 ILCS 14 (Strongest in the Nation)

740 ILCS 14/15(b) — Collection Requirements

No entity may collect a biometric identifier — including facial geometry captured by smart glasses — without first: (1) informing the subject in writing; (2) stating the specific purpose; (3) receiving a written release. Verbal notice alone is insufficient.

740 ILCS 14/20 — Private Right of Action (No Harm Required)

Any aggrieved person may sue in state court and recover statutory damages without proving actual harm. Confirmed in Rosenbach v. Six Flags (2019). Facebook settled for $650M. Google settled for $100M.

California — Civ. Code § 1708.8 & CPRA

Cal. Civ. Code § 1708.8 — Constructive Invasion of Privacy

Capturing video or audio of a person in a private setting through a device — even from a lawful location — without consent. Civil damages up to $5,000 per violation.

Cal. Civ. Code §§ 1798.100–1798.199 (CPRA)

Biometric data requires explicit opt-in consent. Right to know, delete, and opt out. Violations: $100–$750 per consumer per incident or actual damages, whichever is greater.

Cal. Penal Code § 632 — All-Party Consent

California requires consent of all parties for recording confidential communications. Criminal offense without consent.

Texas — CUBI Act & Penal Code § 16.02

Tex. Bus. & Com. Code § 503.001 — CUBI Act

No person may capture a biometric identifier — including face geometry — for commercial purposes without first informing the person and obtaining consent.

Penalties

AG civil action up to $25,000 per knowing violation. AG has actively pursued CUBI enforcement against Google and Meta.

Tex. Penal Code § 16.02 — Unlawful Interception

Intentional interception of oral or electronic communications without consent is a third-degree felony.

Washington — My Health MY Data Act (2023)

RCW 70.372 — My Health MY Data Act

One of the broadest biometric privacy laws in the US. Requires affirmative authorization before collecting, sharing, or selling consumer health data including biometric data. Private right of action available.

RCW 9.73.030 — All-Party Consent

All-party consent required for recording private conversations. Gross misdemeanor — up to 1 year imprisonment.

New York — Civil Rights §§ 50–51 & NYC Biometric Privacy

N.Y. Civil Rights Law §§ 50–51

No entity may use the name, portrait, picture, or voice of any living person without prior written consent. Actual damages, punitive damages, and attorney's fees available.

NYC Admin. Code § 22-1201

Commercial establishments in NYC must post conspicuous notice before collecting biometric data. $500/day per location + $500 per person whose data was collected without notice.

N.Y. Penal Law § 250.45 — Unlawful Surveillance

Recording a person without consent in a private setting is a Class E felony. Distribution elevates to Class D felony.

Florida — All-Party Consent & Video Voyeurism

Fla. Stat. § 934.03 — Security of Communications Act

Florida is an all-party consent state. Intentional interception without consent of all parties is a felony of the third degree — up to 5 years imprisonment.

Fla. Stat. § 810.145 — Video Voyeurism

Recording a person without consent in a location where there is a reasonable expectation of privacy is a felony. Escalates to second-degree felony for repeat offenders.

Oregon — All-Party Consent & Consumer Privacy Act (2024)

ORS § 165.540 — All-Party Consent

No person may obtain any part of a conversation without consent of all participants. Class A misdemeanor — up to 1 year and $6,250 fine.

ORS § 659A.800 — Oregon Consumer Privacy Act

Rights to access, deletion, and opt-out of processing of personal data including biometric data. AG enforcement up to $25,000 per violation.
